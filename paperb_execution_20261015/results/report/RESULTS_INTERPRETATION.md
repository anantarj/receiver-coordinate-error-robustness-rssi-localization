# Interpretation of the completed bounded studies

The two new studies materially broaden the evidence. Calibration-dependent sensitivity is observed in both WCL and a specified finite multistart RSS-residual least-squares estimator, and it persists when calibration is geographically separated and restricted to ten receptions per receiver. The evidence also rejects a simple claim that recalibration uniformly improves accuracy. The resulting contribution is an evaluation problem whose magnitude and sign depend on the estimator, calibration information, geometry and reported statistic.

## What is now established by the evaluated conditions

The catalogue-map two-target 5 km stress shows substantial changes in both error and prediction sensitivity. WCL field-median P50 changes from 892.6 m frozen to 560.1 m refitted; RSS-NLS changes from 841.7 to 572.3 m. Their own unperturbed P50 values are 561.2 and 551.4 m. P95 changes from 4053.5 to 2037.6 m for WCL and 2791.6 to 1945.8 m for RSS-NLS. These are medians of four field-specific quantiles, not quantiles of pooled repeated observations.

On the 371 selected-target-affected messages, field-median prediction movement relative to each estimator's own zero-map baseline falls from 481.5 to 64.3 m for WCL and from 507.3 to 182.1 m for RSS-NLS. For WCL, every affected message has smaller net movement in all four of these fields. RSS-NLS has smaller movement for 69.5–86.3% of affected messages, depending on direction. The broader sensitivity effect therefore transfers, while the row-level response differs. The WCL suppression theorem should remain explicitly WCL-specific; these results do not prove an identical mechanism for RSS-NLS.

Changing calibration policy reverses the full-cohort between-estimator P50 ordering in 11/24 catalogue-map fields and 1/24 historical-map fields. The catalogue reversals have absolute frozen margins of 5.6–103.8 m and refitted margins of 8.1–99.1 m. One 1 km two-target field changes the WCL-minus-RSS-NLS P50 difference from −70.3 to +99.1 m. Thus the result is more informative than a count alone, but not every reversal implies a large application consequence. Neither map has a full-cohort P95 ordering reversal: 0/24 each. The map-specific samples share 505 of 512 rows, so differences in their counts are not a controlled map-only contrast. These cross-estimator counts must remain distinct from the earlier within-WCL parameter rankings.

## The adverse outcomes are part of the explanation

At 1 km, catalogue-map two-target P50 calibration benefits range from −80.4 to +113.1 m for WCL and −9.4 to +207.0 m for RSS-NLS. Under spatial withholding with all training data, RSS-NLS recalibration worsens P50 in 12/16 fields and P95 in 11/16; the direction-median P50 benefit is negative in all four regions. This is evidence against promoting recalibration as a generally beneficial repair procedure.

At 5 km, all 16 all-data and all 48 limited-budget field/fit combinations per estimator have lower P95 under recalibration. Yet P50 worsens in six of the 48 limited-budget WCL combinations and seven RSS-NLS combinations. The same intervention can therefore reduce an upper-tail statistic while worsening the centre of the distribution. This is a stronger narrative than listing threshold crossings without scale or application meaning.

More calibration records do not uniformly improve accuracy. In the northwest region, RSS-NLS own-baseline P50 is 947.1 m with all data and 864.3–940.7 m across the three limited fits. This does not demonstrate that ten receptions are optimal, that fewer data are inherently better, or that one explanation such as spatial mismatch has been isolated. It establishes that the information-state comparison changes starting accuracy as well as adaptation, which is why every budget retains its own baseline. Two budgets cannot identify a learning curve or a minimum sufficient number of references.

## Practical and numerical boundaries

The limited budgets require 297–319 distinct labelled source messages per fold/fit, not ten labelled messages overall. Availability on the full withheld populations ranges from 99.37% to 100%; 500/500 finite outputs in each sampled arm is a different statistic. The four held-out regions belong to one campaign with overlapping training pools, so no independent-deployment claim follows.

The primary arrays contain all 195,200 scheduled RSS-NLS policy-state entries, including duplicate zero-policy entries, and no nonfinite predictions. There are 186,176 entries with each zero baseline counted once and 122,336 unique numerical inputs after exact caching. All 1,304 analysis checks passed. The independent numerical audit completed 376 preselected states with no exceptions or escalation. Stronger optimization moved a prediction by at most 0.0200444 m and improved the objective by at most 3.1837e−9 dB². Only 16 states met the requested 0.01 dB² gap tolerance; 360 remained unresolved, with median gap 0.1054 and maximum 0.7420 dB². This supports the stability of the audited finite-algorithm results while preserving the boundary against claiming global optima. It is not a random estimate of optimizer failure prevalence.

The manuscript can now lead with matched estimator and information-state evidence, use the WCL proof to explain one mechanism, and use adverse results to delimit that explanation. The remaining environmental boundary is one measured deployment. All results concern the recorded RSSI, reference positions and supplied maps under controlled metadata interventions; they do not establish how frequently such interventions occur in deployed systems.
